\begin{abstract}
This report will document a mathematical and computational closure program
spanning particle-to-kinetic, wave-to-kinetic, kinetic-to-fluid, and
application-scale reductions motivated by TEMPEST.  It will compare classical
Markov closures, explicit cumulant and memory models, physics-informed neural
networks, neural operators, physics-informed neural operators, learned
closures, structured learned dynamics, and action-conditioned world models.
The experiment is organized as a linked sequence of effective-interaction,
particle-to-kinetic, kinetic-to-moment, and application-transfer tests rather
than a single architecture leaderboard. The application profiles are a
dusty-plasma many-body closure ladder and a core-collapse-supernova turbulence
closure that transfers unresolved 3D effects into a reduced 1D carrier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The organizing question is not which architecture wins in the abstract, but
what information is required to close a reduced model reliably and how that
requirement changes across asymptotic and breakdown regimes.

We built an evaluation harness for computational closure methods: a fixed
suite of test problems and forcing regimes, a common interface through which
any closure model---analytic, learned, or hybrid---is invoked, and a
standardized battery of accuracy, stability, and cost metrics computed
uniformly across candidates. The harness separates the science (the closures)
from the scaffolding (data handling, integration, scoring), so that adding a
new method requires implementing one interface, and every reported comparison
is reproducible and fair by construction.

\section{Mathematical closure framework}
This section will define scale transitions and exact hierarchy defects,
connected correlations and cumulants, interaction histories and finite memory,
and the decomposition of numerical, statistical, asymptotic, closure, and
learning error.

For a representative hierarchy,
\begin{equation}
  f_2=f_1f_1+g_2,
  \qquad
  \partial_t f_1=\mathcal L f_1+\mathcal C[f_1]
  +\mathcal R[g_2,g_3,\ldots],
\end{equation}
where the closure problem is to approximate the residual $\mathcal R$ from a
declared information set while preserving the required mathematical structure.

\section{Canonical scale-limit benchmarks}
The canonical track is motivated by the long-time scale-limit program of
Zaher Hani and collaborators. It contains hard-particle dynamics to
Boltzmann, nonlinear wave dynamics to the wave kinetic equation, and
Boltzmann dynamics to Euler and Navier--Stokes--Fourier. These are not
ready-made TEMPEST closures. They are benchmark regimes in which the
microscopic and reduced equations, asymptotic coordinates, expected limit,
and discarded information are all explicit.

This makes them the validation layer for the computational evaluation
harness. The particle track exposes connected cumulants, recollisions, and
collision ancestry; the wave track exposes spectral cumulants and resonant
interaction histories; and the kinetic-to-fluid track exposes
non-Maxwellian moments and constitutive remainders. Each benchmark will test
the trusted asymptotic regime, controlled breakdown outside its assumptions,
long-time behavior, and separation of numerical, ensemble, asymptotic, and
closure-model errors. TEMPEST applications retain this diagnostic protocol
but replace the equations, states, constraints, observables, and coefficients.

\section{Experimental pipeline}
For every transition the experiment declares a high-fidelity state $X$, a
projection $\Pi$, a retained state $u=\Pi X$, and a trusted reduced operator
$F_0$. The exact or estimable closure defect is
\begin{equation}
  C^\star[X]=\Pi F(X)-F_0(\Pi X).
\end{equation}
The specification names the discarded particles, fields, correlations,
moments, and histories whose effect appears in $C^\star$, together with the
information available to each candidate during training and inference.

The complete execution sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item predeclare states, scaling coordinates, forcing regimes, splits,
    observables, information budgets, uncertainty estimators, and gates;
  \item verify high-fidelity and reduced references under numerical refinement
    and mandatory physical checks;
  \item generate versioned ensembles and project them into marginals, moments,
    cumulants, interaction histories, and closure defects;
  \item fit matched analytic, learned, and hybrid candidates through the common
    model interface;
  \item test the learned object a priori on held-out targets and then embed
    closures in the carrier solver for a-posteriori stability and feedback
    tests;
  \item run predeclared asymptotic, controlled-breakdown,
    out-of-distribution, and cross-profile experiments without test-time
    retuning; and
  \item audit every claim, retain failed runs, decompose numerical, sampling,
    estimator, asymptotic, model, and rollout error, and generate the report
    from machine-readable results.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Four linked bake-offs}
The first stage learns an effective force at the retained-particle level after
ions, electrons, wakes, fields, collisions, or faster dynamics have been
eliminated. It compares analytic interaction laws, identifiable pair kernels,
physics-tailored force decompositions, contextual graph models, and
finite-memory auxiliary-state models. This is a closure when the force
represents eliminated environmental degrees of freedom; it is not yet a
kinetic or fluid closure.

The second stage projects verified particle ensembles to a one-particle
distribution. It compares classical mean-field or kinetic operators, explicit
correlation and cumulant corrections, weak-form discovered operators, learned
kinetic residuals, memory models, and neural operators or PINOs when paired
solution families are available. Particle cumulants, factorization error,
hierarchy residuals, collective modes, transport, and long kinetic rollouts
define the evidence.

The third stage projects kinetic solutions to density, momentum, energy, and a
predeclared set of additional moments. Classical constitutive laws, Grad or
maximum-entropy closures, hyperbolic learned moment closures, explicit
cumulant and memory states, and structure-preserving learned residuals are
tested on stress and heat flux, realizability, hyperbolicity, conservation,
entropy behavior, classical-limit recovery, and embedded-solver stability.

The fourth stage transfers only validated diagnostic logic, state-selection
rules, structural parameterizations, uncertainty procedures, and stopping
criteria to a TEMPEST application. Coefficients, weights, state variables, and
theorem assumptions remain application-specific unless a separate experiment
justifies their transfer.

\subsection{Cross-cutting pair, context, and memory experiment}
The proposed dusty-plasma reference family contains heterogeneous particles,
confinement, drag, screened repulsion, nonreciprocal wake effects, external
forcing, and an optional dynamical wake or field coordinate $\zeta_j$:
\begin{equation}
  m_i\dot v_i=F_i^{\mathrm{conf}}+F_i^{\mathrm{ext}}-\gamma_i v_i
  +\sum_{j\ne i}F_{ij}(r_i,r_j,\zeta_j;\mu)+\eta_i,
  \qquad
  \tau_w\dot\zeta_j=\zeta_{\mathrm{eq}}(\mathcal E_j)-\zeta_j.
\end{equation}
The actual reference physics must be selected with the scientific owner. The
design sweeps wake relaxation $\tau_w$, density or coupling, screening,
heterogeneity, and forcing history. The same data compare an analytic pair
law, learned pair kernel, instantaneous many-particle graph model,
finite-memory graph or auxiliary-state model, and unrestricted black-box
control. The experiment locates where pairwise information stops being
sufficient, where many-particle context becomes necessary, and where explicit
memory earns its cost. Projected cumulants and interaction-history complexity
are tested as early warnings of those boundaries. The resulting ensembles
then feed the particle-to-kinetic and kinetic-to-moment stages.

\section{TEMPEST application profiles}
The dusty-plasma profile retains observed particles while eliminating plasma,
wake, field, and sub-observation-time degrees of freedom. Its nested pair,
contextual graph, and finite-memory candidates locate when each information
class becomes necessary. The same verified ensembles then supply
particle-to-kinetic and kinetic-to-moment closure targets.

The second science application is core-collapse-supernova turbulence. A 3D
radiation hydrodynamics or (M)HD reference is projected by a declared angular
or volume average, Favre/Reynolds convention, radial binning, filter, and shock
alignment into a reduced 1D carrier. The exact closure defect is
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{SN}}^\star[X]
  =\langle F_{3\mathrm D}(X)\rangle_\Omega
  -F_{1\mathrm D}(\langle X\rangle_\Omega),
\end{equation}
decomposed into Reynolds stress or turbulent pressure, turbulent energy and
enthalpy flux, production, dissipation, mixing, and optional finite-memory
terms. The initial candidates are an unaugmented 1D carrier, a declared local
RANS/mixing-length or STIR-like baseline, explicit turbulent-state
augmentations, structure-preserving learned residuals, and finite-memory
corrections. Each is tested after embedding on shock-radius evolution,
revival/runaway timing, turbulent support, conservative energy and lepton
accounting, realizability, explosion energy, and nucleosynthesis-sensitive
thermodynamic histories.

\section{Methods and information budgets}
Methods will include classical, cumulant, and memory closures; PINNs, neural
operators, and PINOs; learned closures and structured dynamics; and
action-conditioned world models. Privileged information and matched
comparisons will be reported explicitly. PINNs are evaluated as
equation-informed approximations of particular forward or inverse solutions;
neural operators and PINOs target parameterized solution maps; learned
closures target an identified residual inside a retained solver; and a world
model is admitted only for a genuine sequential intervention. Direct rankings
are restricted to candidates approximating the same object.

\section{Experimental protocol}
The protocol will cover reference-solver verification, ensemble and estimator
uncertainty, a-priori closure tests, a-posteriori embedded-solver tests, and
transfer and failure-boundary experiments. Separate scorecards report target
accuracy and identifiability, dynamics and reliability, closure diagnosis,
physical and mathematical validity, limit recovery and transfer, and total
information and computational cost. Unlike tasks are not averaged into one
overall winner.

\section{Results}
Results will report recovery of classical limits; cumulant and memory
diagnostics; predictive and embedded closure performance; dusty-plasma and
supernova application-profile performance; and compute and information
efficiency.

\section{Discussion}
The discussion will ask which closure information is necessary, what transfers
from canonical and dusty-plasma systems to the supernova profile, and where
negative results limit generalization.

\section{Reproducibility statement}
The reproducibility record will link data and result manifests, code,
configurations, environments, claim levels, and retained failures.
